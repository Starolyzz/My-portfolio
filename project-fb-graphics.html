The Brief

Different menus, different rooms, one steady hand.

Across a handful of food & beverage clients — Casa Kenji, Aya Sushi, Copper Espresso Cafe, and a few smaller one-off jobs — the ask was always the same shape: turn a promo, an event, an hours change, or a new menu item into something people would actually stop scrolling for.

Casa Kenji needed that late-night Latin energy, Aya Sushi wanted a cleaner omakase feel, and Copper leaned cozy café warmth. None of it was one template reused four ways. Every post was built around the specific brand it was for.

The Process

Read the brand before you open the software.

Jumping between four different F&B brands in the same week meant the process had to start the same way every time: look at what the brand had already posted, figure out what "on-brand" actually meant for them, then design inside those lines.

  • Most of these were tied to a specific date — an event, a holiday, an hours change — so there wasn't room to sit on a draft. Lock it, export it, move on.
  • Feed posts, story graphics, and the one print piece for Copper all needed their own crop and file setup. Nothing carries over cleanly between those three.
  • Casa Kenji's Latin Night series, Aya Sushi's calmer omakase style, and Copper's warmer café tone had to look like they came from three different studios, because in a sense they did.

The Outcome

Small jobs, real reps.

27 pieces across four clients — feed posts, story graphics, and one printed piece — each one built quickly, on-brand, and ready to post the same day it was needed.

These are the jobs that don't make it into a big case study on their own, but doing four brand voices well in the same week is its own kind of practice.

Every client here came back for more than one post, which is its own small proof that the work was landing.
