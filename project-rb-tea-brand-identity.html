The Brief

One brand, growing into every format.

R&B Tea Houston had an existing social presence, but the posts felt generic — there was no clear visual identity tying them together. My job was to bring coherence and personality to their feed.

The relationship grew from there. Their in-house TV menus needed a refresh, then their car meet events needed promotional flyers, and I just stayed on — carrying the same visual language into whatever new format the brand picked up next.

The Process

Pinterest-first, brand-always.

My process typically started on Pinterest — gathering references that matched the mood, colour palette, or season of the upcoming piece before touching any design software. This kept the work visually current without losing the brand's own character, whether it was landing in a phone feed, on a screen ten feet away, or printed on a flyer.

Three principles guided every piece:

  • The palette had to make sense for both the brand and whatever product or theme was on deck that week.
  • Fonts got locked in early and I didn't touch them again — same type, every format, no exceptions.
  • The TV menus were the real test: real product photography and a hierarchy you could actually read from across the room, not just up close on a phone.

The Outcome

One identity, every touchpoint.

There's a steady stream of feed content now — product features, promos, holiday posts. TV menus run across locations in both horizontal and vertical formats. And a run of car meet flyers made it out into print and social for actual event crowds, not just the feed.

What I'm most proud of is how this grew from a handful of Instagram posts into a full, multi-format identity — feed content, screen menus, and event materials that all still feel like one brand.

Every piece started with the brand, ended with the brief, and found its own visual personality somewhere in between.
